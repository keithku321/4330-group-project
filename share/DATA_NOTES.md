| Chunk size | top-k | Hit rate | nDCG | Faithfulness | Answer relevancy | Token F1 | Prompt tok/query | Latency (s) |

| 600 | 20 | 0.935 | 0.793 | 0.926 | 0.842 | 0.364 | 12,408 | 18.2 |
| 600 | 40 | 0.804 | 0.748 | 0.925 | 0.828 | 0.367 | 12,424 | 17.8 |

| 1200 | 40 (default) | 0.913 | 0.833 | 0.895 | 0.857 | 0.359 | 12,206 | 17.4 |

* top-k is the number of entities and relations retrieved; `chunk_top_k` stayed at 20 and the context budget at 12,000 tokens. With top-k 40 the entity and relation text takes more of that fixed budget, which likely leaves less room for chunks. That would explain why hit rate falls most at chunk 600 / top-k 40 (0.804), but we did not check it directly.
* Index cost at chunk 600: **196 chunks, 322 minutes, 405 LLM calls, 1,435,599 prompt + 665,888 completion tokens** (2.10M total), about twice the chunk-1200 cost. This index ran in one go, so unlike the chunk-1200 figure it is not a lower bound.
* nDCG at each chunk size uses that chunk size's chunk count per document, so nDCG across chunk sizes is only roughly comparable.

## Corpus and indexing

* Source: UltraDomain `mix.jsonl`. We kept the shortest documents until about 100k tokens.
* **20 documents, 95,302 tokens (cl100k), 67,078 words, 94 chunks** (1200 tokens, 100 overlap).
* **46 questions:** qasper 19, 2WikiMQA 16, multifieldqa 8, HotpotQA 3.
* LightRAG index: **161 minutes, 156 LLM calls, 681,579 prompt + 334,531 completion tokens** (1.02M total). Resulting graph: 4,311 nodes, 2,652 edges.
* These index figures are for the **resumed run only**. The first attempt was stopped after it had processed 2 documents, and LightRAG reused its cached extraction output, so the true total is somewhat higher. Treat 161 min / 156 calls as a lower bound.
* Entity extraction was cut off by the output cap on many chunks. Per-document log lines such as "truncation hit 4 of 6 chunks" appear for all 20 documents (20 log lines). Summed over those lines this is roughly 60 of 94 chunks, but the log spans two runs, so do not quote an exact number.

From the next export on, `export.py` adds six columns: `rag_model` (the model that wrote the answer) and `judge_model` (the model that judged it) after `mode`, and `faithfulness_sd`, `faithfulness_n`, `answer_relevancy_sd`, `answer_relevancy_n` after each RAGAS score (the spread over the repeated judgings and how many judgings the mean uses). * **`judge_correct` does not exist.** We never ran a correctness judge, so there is nothing to put there. The closest columns are `token_f1` (overlap with the gold answer) and the two RAGAS scores. Do not describe any of them as "accuracy".
* `experiment` is `exp1`, `exp2` or `exp3`; `system` is unique per row group (e.g. `lightrag-mix-c600-k20`); `mode` is `naive_rag` or `lightrag-<query mode>`. To compare Exp 2 settings, filter on `chunk_size` and `top_k`; the 1200 / 40 setting is the `exp3` `lightrag-mix` rows.
* `answer` is the raw model output (LightRAG answers still contain the trailing "References" list; it was stripped before scoring).

## Numbers we are not fully sure about (word these carefully)

1. **LightRAG faithfulness (0.829) is probably underestimated.** It was judged against the retrieved chunk text only, but LightRAG also gave the LLM entity and relation descriptions. The local Exp 2 and Exp 3 runs (and the notebook) judge against both; the Exp 1 numbers were not recomputed. Compare Exp 3 `mix` (0.895) for the fairer figure.
2. **Entity-level retrieval was often missing.** For 16 of 46 questions qwen2.5:14b returned no low-level keywords (high-level ones were always present), so for those questions LightRAG's mix run used relation-level retrieval plus chunk search only.
3. **Truncated extraction** means the knowledge graph is incomplete, which probably hurts LightRAG.
4. **n = 46, one run, no repeated trials.** The small differences (answer relevancy 0.859 vs 0.834, nDCG 0.833 vs 0.821) should not be called significant.
5. **Same model writes and judges the answers, and judges inconsistently.** These numbers were judged once by qwen2.5:14b, the model that wrote the answers, so RAGAS scores may be biased; a repeat run also showed this judge is not consistent with itself (faithfulness differed on 22 of the 42 questions whose answers were word for word the same). **The pipeline now fixes both problems** (README, "The judge"): a third-party judge, DeepSeek through DeepSeek Harness, removes the self-judgement, and every score is the mean of 3 judgings by default (`LIGHTRAG_JUDGE_REPEATS`, 3 to 5), with its spread recorded, which addresses the repeatability. The smoke test showed both parts are needed: one DeepSeek judging alone still moved faithfulness by 0.155 on average, while a mean of three varies about 1.7 times less. **The numbers in this folder have not been rescored with the fixed judge yet**; once they are, `run_info.json` and the `judge_model` column will name the DeepSeek judge, and the old and new scores must not be mixed.
6. **Hit rate and nDCG are document level**, not passage level. Hit rate is high for both systems because the corpus has only 20 documents.
7. **Index cost is a lower bound** (see above).
